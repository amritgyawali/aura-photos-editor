# Hot Features

Open **Hot Features** in the sidebar. Choose one of the five cards, then select a photograph in the filmstrip. Adding photos from this section imports them without automatically editing them. Apply or discard a precision-retouch draft before switching tools or photographs.

| Tool | Workflow |
| --- | --- |
| Liquify Background Repair | Make a face/body shape or liquify edit, then choose **Repair background automatically**. Adjust **Background repair** from 0 to 100. Requires a detected subject and background; use the Restore brush for manual repairs. |
| Face Freckles & Acne | **Acne · keep freckles** starts with skin-only healing, protected moles and retained pore texture. **Acne & freckles** explicitly includes dark marks. Run automatic retouch on the photograph or collection, compare, inspect saved coverage, and paint remaining spots with the Blemish brush. |
| Remove Glasses Glare | The native Glare tool opens selected with live preview. Multiple lenses can have separate saved operations. |

The desktop now trains from real local RAW/export pairs rather than an empty fixture source. Stop learning requests cancellation after the current pair. Training is local. |

## Native background repair

`studio_finish_v1.backgroundRepair` is a finite amount from 0 to 100. Zero is omitted when serialized, preserving older recipe encodings. Repair resamples the frame in two layers. The subject layer follows the combined face/body/liquify displacement field exactly. The background layer is a plate of the pre-warp background (current colour, retouch and light edits included) in which the subject's footprint is filled from the surrounding background by push-pull interpolation. Its displacement follows the subject at the subject's edge and relaxes smoothly to `1 - amount` of the field further out, over a distance of at least 3% of the frame and 2.5 times the largest displacement. If that transition would fold the background anywhere the warp itself does not (heavily stacked strokes can), it is widened in steps; if no width is safe, the background follows the warp, which is the unrepaired result. Repair therefore never doubles background content. A very large brush near the subject leaves most of the background inside the transition, so it is only partly straightened there. At 100, background lines beyond that short transition are exactly where they were; near the subject they bend smoothly instead of tearing into a doubled contour or leaving a seam at an uncertain, soft boundary. Pixels the field does not move are returned unchanged. The subject/background blend uses the segmentation confidence sampled at the warped source. Undo restores the previous recipe; export uses the same renderer. The render engine version includes `background-repair.2` to invalidate old cached results.

The save command refuses enabling/changing repair when it cannot find both a foreground subject and a background. A zero setting remains available to disable it. Segmentation quality still affects boundary quality. It cannot recover pixels missing from an imported flattened liquify edit without a clean original. Glare softening cannot reconstruct an eye hidden by opaque or clipped reflections. Skin-mark classification and single-reference colour transfer also require review; no universal perfect result is claimed.

Cards use local vector artwork before a photo is selected and the selected photo's cached thumbnail afterward. These are tool illustrations, not fabricated before/after results; working comparisons are inside the native editor. No external stock-image requests or customer photographs are bundled.

## Qualification and remaining limits (2026-10-25)

The saved subject matte now participates in the same native background-repair availability check used by rendering. Changing that matte rechecks support even when repair strength is unchanged. Glare estimates the scene under the reflection from pixels outside the selection only (a feathered selection's soft edge counts as unknown, so the reflection never becomes part of its own estimate), filled inward by push-pull. The veil is the rise of each pixel's darkest channel above that estimate, averaged over the pixels where the veil is visible (so an iris beside a measurement does not pull it down) and limited to twice the pixel's own rise; a 12% tolerance decides where it is visible but is not subtracted. Where the reflection crosses something darker than the lens's surroundings (iris, pupil, lashes), the veil cannot be seen directly, so the measured veil is carried across gaps that the reflection encloses (found by flood fill from outside the selection, never across a notch open to the outside), with zero veil known outside the selection. A pixel clearly darker than the veil there is treated as being in front of the lens and kept exactly. The tint is the per-channel median of each pixel's rise over its darkest channel's rise, pulled toward neutral when the ratios disagree. The feather only softens the selection's outer edge, so removal is even across the reflection. Clipped detail cannot be recovered and lands on the surrounding tone; it does not reconstruct eyes hidden by opaque glare. Auto advanced retouch's jewellery stage uses the same tool at a quarter strength, which takes a burnt-out highlight down by about a sixth. Its renderer change adds `glare.3` to cache identity.

Measured with `crates/aura-render/tests/glare_photos.rs` (a known synthetic reflection, neutral, green-coated and purple-coated, added to real eyes and removed): 46-57% of the reflection removed on both eyes of one portrait and 74-85% on the shadow-side eye of another, against 3-41% for the previous softening. The hard case is an eye socket much darker than the brow and cheek around the lens (11-35% removed): the reflection barely rises above its surroundings there. Smooth skin shading under a reflection is indistinguishable from a smooth veil, so it is flattened toward the surrounding tone; compare before applying.

Look training persists the scanned folder selection in the local catalogue. It resolves unique file names, decodes RAWs into the renderer's working space, colour-manages delivered images and fits each accepted pair through the actual renderer. The new desktop path does not trust unconfirmed sidecar edits. It requires at least 120 unique file-name pairs and retains the engine's separate profile usability/adoption gate. Duplicate names across folders are refused rather than arbitrarily assigned. Identical image content under different file names is not deduplicated by this adapter.

Archive training currently uses the neutral baseline and default scene/light context; this does not qualify a scene-conditioned model. Crops, unsupported camera inputs, local/composite edits and grades outside the fitter can be rejected. Resume retains measured pairs; rescan before training again if exports change. Scanning is not cancellable; fitting stops between pairs. Successful training remains a candidate, and a candidate that fails the usability gate cannot be adopted from this panel.

Profile selection reads the fresh project status and drops superseded requests. A newly trained candidate is explicitly selected. Profile actions cannot overlap, and a cancelled result does not surface an older profile as newly trained. The detailed runtime report records both successes and unqualified cases.

## Finding lens reflections automatically (2026-10-26)

`find_glare` (`crates/aura-render/src/glare_find.rs`, command in `portrait_commands.rs`) proposes glare selections; the panel turns them into glare operations in one history step, so one Undo removes them all. Nothing is applied without the person pressing the button, and every proposal can be adjusted or deleted.

For each face the portrait parse finds (eye spacing at least 28 px), a lens-sized window around each eye is searched. Seeds are pixels well above the bright end of that person's own skin and clearly less colourful than it; a blob grows from them into the weaker part of the same reflection. A blob is a reflection only when it reaches beyond the eye opening (an eye opening sized from the landmarks, so catchlights and eye whites are refused even where the eye mask misses them), lies mostly on the central part of the lens (not the cheek, brow, temple, ear or nose, which is never a candidate), is big enough for the face, ends with a crisp edge (a sheen on skin fades out), and stands out from the skin right around it. Finally the eye must show the look of a spectacle rim: steps well above the face's own skin texture in most directions below and beside the eye, where a lens rim runs. Overlapping proposals (a face parsed twice) are merged so a reflection is never removed twice.

Measured on the 1,000-photo wedding set (`F:\AURA-Indian-Wedding-Test\source`, licences in its manifest) with `crates/aura-render/tests/glare_find_photos.rs` (ignored; `AURA_FIND_DIR` of `NAME_WxH.rgb`, writes `findings.json` with every blob considered and why it was kept or refused): on 64 held-out proposals labelled by eye before scoring, the rim check raised precision from 50% to 78% while keeping 66% of the true reflections. Six glasses-free portraits gave no proposals. In the real window (`scripts/test-glare-finder-desktop.py`) a guest's lens glints were found within 0.003 of where they are, removed, and restored by one Undo; a clean portrait reported nothing and saved nothing.

Limits: faint, broad lens sheen that does not stand out from the skin is left alone; a strong reflection that hides the eye can shift the face parse's eye landmarks and then be missed; eyelid creases and eye make-up can imitate a rim, so roughly one proposal in five is still not a lens reflection; compare before keeping it.

## AI Color Match on another scene (2026-10-26)

Tried and reverted: applying the reference's neutral-axis cast on top of the target's own cast (instead of replacing it) when the reference shows another scene. On the synthetic batch check it stopped an already-warm photograph from moving away from the known grade (1.28 to 1.79 became 1.28 to 0.94). But in the real window a match onto a different scene then measured no improvement in appearance, the non-regression check kept the photograph unchanged, and the button appeared to do nothing. The tool's job is to make a photograph look like the reference, so the replacing cast stays.

The two accuracy gates that ask an unrelated-scene match to close 40% of the gap to a known, hidden grade (`a_batch_matched_to_one_reference_converges_on_one_look`, `a_new_photograph_takes_an_edited_references_look_through_the_renderer`) still fail, as they did at commit 8a7866f: a single edited reference cannot separate its grade from its scene's own colours, and matching appearance is a different goal from recovering a hidden edit. Their thresholds are unchanged.

## When there is no person (2026-10-27)

Face, body, skin, makeup and people adjustments act only where the portrait parse found a face
(or the photographer drew one in Portrait retouch). On a photograph with nobody in it - a still
life, an empty venue, wildlife - they leave the photograph unchanged, and saving one now returns a
note that the finishing panel shows above its tools, so a slider never appears to be broken.
Background adjustments need no person and are never reported. `save_studio_finish` carries the
note in `notes`; the parse it checks is the renderer's own, so the note agrees with the render.
