## Purpose

Decide whether a soft region is a mistake.

The three classical measures in `crates/aura-brain-photo/src/integrity/focus.rs` - the
Laplacian variance, the Tenengrad and the contrast-normalised acutance - already answer
"how much high-frequency energy is in this region", and they answer it well. PHASE-09
section 6.1 names the four cases where that is the wrong question:

> shallow depth of field, veil textures, backlit rim light, heavy bokeh.

Every one of those is a case where the measurement is **right about the pixels and wrong
about the photograph**. A lace veil genuinely carries more high-frequency energy than an
in-focus cheek. Creamy background bokeh genuinely carries almost none. A classical
measure that reports those faithfully will call the veil sharp and the f/1.4 portrait
soft, and section 13's second acceptance criterion is that the second of those must never
happen.

So the head is a **classifier over interpretations** rather than a sharpness regressor. A
regressor would be asked to output a number the classical measure already produces, and
would learn to reproduce its mistakes.

## Architecture

A small convolutional stem into a three-way softmax:

```
crop [N, 1, 64, 64]
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (16ch) -> Relu          # 64 -> 32
  -> MaxPool 2x2                          # 32 -> 16
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (32ch) -> Relu          # 16 -> 8
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 32x32 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 3x32 -> Softmax -> focus_probs
```

Three decisions in it are deliberate.

**Single-channel input.** Focus is a luminance question, the analyser already holds the
luminance plane from `aura_vision::embed::descriptors::luma_plane`, and a three-channel
input would triple the first convolution to carry chroma the question does not use.

**64 px crops.** The head is asked about the *character* of the edges, not about their
finest detail - the classical measures are already computing the finest detail at full
proxy resolution. 64 px makes it cheap enough to run on several regions of every frame
inside section 11's 45 ms budget.

**Slot 2 exists.** A two-class head would have to put creamy background bokeh somewhere,
and "soft" is the only place it could go. `FocusClass::Bokeh` is what keeps section 13's
second criterion satisfiable by the model as well as by the region geometry.

### The asymmetry, which is the most important thing on this card

`focus::apply_head` lets the head **withdraw** a softness claim and never lets it make
one:

* a confident `Bokeh` (>= `HEAD_OVERRULES_AT`, 0.70) sets `shallow_depth_of_field`, which
  suppresses a `SUBJECT_SOFT` reason and adds an exoneration;
* a confident `Soft` does **nothing**. The flag is raised by the classical measurement
  against the scene's threshold or not at all.

Section 12's first failure mode is that false rejections destroy trust instantly. A
placeholder model that could convict a photograph would be the fastest possible route to
one. Lifting the asymmetry after training is an ADR and a re-validation, not a constant
change.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::focus_head`. On a real crop it produces a distribution over
three classes that describes a random projection of the pixels.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-09-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger**, shared
with `eye_state`.

The reason is the one phases 05, 06 and 07 all give: there is no labelled wedding data in
this repository, no consented dataset and no GPU backend. Section 8 step 3 asks DATA to
"label sharp/soft crops" and section 9 gives them 20,000 crops across four label
families; that work has not been done.

What the day a real dataset arrives looks like:

1. `ml/models/integrity/train_focus.py --build-dataset` samples crops from the catalog at
   the same 64 px scale the analyser uses, stratified by **region kind** - eye, face,
   body, background band - because the class balance is wildly different in each and a
   uniform sample would be 80 % background.
2. **Wedding-level splits, never frame-level.** Two frames from the same burst three
   hundred milliseconds apart are near-duplicates; a frame-level split puts one in train
   and one in test and reports an accuracy that measures leakage.
3. Label from three sources rather than one: focus-bracketed pairs give sharp/soft for
   free, the photographer's own keep/reject gives a weak signal, and a human labels the
   ambiguous middle - which is where the whole value is.
4. **The bokeh class needs deliberate mining.** Random sampling produces almost none of
   it; the examples come from wide-aperture frames where the *face* region is sharp and
   the background band is not, which is exactly the geometry `RegionSet` already computes.
5. `eval_integrity.py --auc` measures section 10.1's subject-focus AUC on a held-out set,
   and the gate in `tests/eval/integrity_eval.rs` is re-pointed from the synthetic blur
   ladder at real crops.

## Quality gate

Section 10.1's focus thresholds, and what each is measured against today.

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic blur ladder is monotonic | strict | `fixtures::blur_ladder`, seven rungs | **met** |
| Back and front focus correctly signed | exact | `fixtures::focus_miss` | **met** |
| Subject-focus AUC | >= 0.96 | - | **deferred (C1): needs labelled crops** |
| Shallow-DOF portraits never flagged soft | exact | `fixtures::shallow_depth_of_field` | **met** |
| Cross-camera fairness, 24 MP vs 61 MP | <= 0.05 | `fixtures::cross_camera_pair` | **met** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

Read the "measured against" column. The rows marked met are real measurements of the
*analysers* - the region order, the normalisation, the sign convention, the calibration
division - against ground truth known by construction. The AUC row is the one that needs
this model's weights, and it is deferred.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and applies
   equally to a trained version.
2. **A region with no contrast produces no opinion worth having.** `RegionSharpness::is_measurable`
   refuses below 3 % contrast and the region is skipped rather than scored; a head asked
   about a blown veil would answer confidently about nothing.
3. **64 px is a real ceiling.** A face 40 px tall in the proxy upsamples into the crop and
   the head sees an interpolation. Phase 06's quality gate already excludes such faces
   from voting on identity; here they simply carry less weight in the prominence mean.
4. **Veil and lace textures remain the hardest class**, trained or not, and they are the
   reason the head exists. A trained version is expected to confuse `sharp` and `soft`
   there more than anywhere else, and the mitigation is the asymmetry above: a wrong
   `soft` costs nothing because it cannot raise a flag.
5. **A re-render invalidates every reading.** The head reads phase 02's documented proxy;
   a `PIPELINE_VER` bump changes the pixels it sees. `image_integrity.model_ver` and the
   proxy's `PIPELINE_VER` are separate columns in separate tables, and `AURA-ML-5033` is
   what stops two vintages being compared.
6. **int8 is permitted here and forbidden on `eye_state`.** The reason is the asymmetry:
   quantisation noise around a threshold that can only ever exonerate a photograph costs
   nothing a photographer would see.

## Fallback

Complete, and it has two layers.

1. **Per frame**: a head that raises is caught by `IntegrityPass::run_ids`, coded
   `AURA-ML-5035`, and **no row is written** - so the frame is retried next pass and is
   never stored as "nothing wrong".
2. **Per measurement**: with the head uninstalled or failing, the classical measures alone
   decide focus. `FocusResult::shallow_depth_of_field` is still set by the *geometric*
   test (`BOKEH_RATIO`, subject 2.2× sharper than the background), so section 13's second
   criterion survives without the model. That is deliberate: the acceptance criterion is
   not allowed to depend on a model that might not load.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets a full technical verdict on every frame.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_integrity` row stale, so the next pass re-analyses; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5033`), and a photographer's dismissals are replayed onto the new verdict rather
than dropped.
