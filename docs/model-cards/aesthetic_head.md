## Purpose

Learn the exchange rate between framing violations, per scene.

PHASE-11 section 6.3 is titled "learned aesthetics, **honestly scoped**", and the scoping
is the design:

> Feature inputs include the geometric measures, so the model learns *how much* each
> violation matters rather than re-deriving geometry.

Everything geometric about a frame is already measured by the time this head runs: the
horizon to a tenth of a degree, the headroom against a scene band, the cuts against the
frame edges, the clutter against a scene tolerance. What four thousand photographer
comparisons can teach - the only thing they can teach at that scale - is how those trade
off against each other: how many degrees of tilt is one cut wrist worth in a couple
portrait, and whether the answer is different on a dance floor.

## Architecture

A three-layer perceptron over thirty-five numbers:

```
features [N, 35]        # 12 geometric + 23 scene one-hot
  -> Gemm 64x35 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 32x64 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 1x32  -> Sigmoid -> aesthetic
```

**It takes no pixels.** A small convolutional head trained on the same data would
rediscover a worse horizon estimator and spend most of its capacity doing so. The twelve
inputs are listed in `composition::aesthetic::Feature`, and that enum **is** the input
order - renumbering it relabels a trained head, exactly as `FaceExpression::channels` and
`Interaction::ALL` do for phase 10.

**The scene is a one-hot, not an index.** A scalar scene index would let the network learn
an ordering between scenes that does not exist - that a `ritual` is somehow between a
`kiss` and a `venue`. Section 2.1 requires a flat-lay to be judged differently from a
couple portrait, not less than one.

**Trained pairwise, shipped pointwise.** The loss compares two frames of the same wedding;
the network scores one. That is the standard Bradley-Terry arrangement phase 10's moment
ranker also uses, and it is why the manifest's task field says `ranker` rather than
`regression`: the absolute value of the output means nothing and only the ordering of two
outputs does. `ml/models/composition/train_aesthetic.py` is where that is written down.

### The cap, which is the most important thing on this card

Section 6.3's last line: "aesthetic score is capped in influence: Phase 12 weights it
below technical integrity and emotion, because taste should break ties, not override
substance."

That cap is **applied here, not left to phase 12 to remember**, and it binds in two places:

* `composition::rules::AESTHETIC_WEIGHT_CAP` (0.50) is the most a rule file may ask for;
  a file that asks for more is *refused* rather than clamped, so whoever wrote it finds
  out.
* `composition::score::AESTHETIC_CAP` (0.25) is the most the composite will grant, and it
  binds underneath the weight. A frame that geometry scored at 0.40 can reach 0.50 with
  perfect taste and falls to 0.30 with none - comfortably enough to decide between two
  technically identical frames of one moment, which typically differ by 0.03 to 0.08, and
  nowhere near enough to lift a frame with a cut neck above one without.

A cap that lives in the consuming phase is a cap the second consuming phase forgets.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.** There are two separate gaps here and they are worth
keeping apart.

**The head's weights** are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::aesthetic_head`. Its output over a real frame is a random
projection of thirty-five numbers. This is **condition C1 of
`docs/progress/PHASE-11-EXIT.md`**, shared with `pose_keypoints`, and it is why section
10.1's pairwise-agreement gate of 0.78 is measured against
`Aesthetic::from_features` - the deterministic twelve-term linear reference model in
`composition::aesthetic` - rather than against this head.

**The reference model's coefficients** are the second gap, and it is the same one phase
10's ranker reports. Section 9 gives DATA "4k composition pairwise comparisons"; there are
none. The twelve coefficients are set from a photographer's argument, and **four of the
twelve are unidentifiable from any data this repository contains** and are set by
reasoning alone: `HorizonConfidence` (weighted at zero, because a confidence is not a
quality), `Headroom`, `NegativeSpace` and `CentreOffset`. That is **condition C2 of the
phase 11 exit report**.

What the day real comparisons arrive looks like:

1. Build the JSONL described by `python ml/models/composition/train_aesthetic.py --plan`,
   sampling pairs **within a moment and within a scene**. Comparing a flat-lay to a dance
   frame teaches the model about scenes, not about framing, and there are only twenty-three
   scenes to learn.
2. **Multi-photographer labels**, which section 12's second failure mode requires: a head
   trained on one photographer's choices encodes one photographer's taste, and personalising
   it is phase 30's job rather than a substitute for diversity here.
3. **Wedding-level splits, never pair-level.** Two pairs sharing a frame leak.
4. Run `python ml/models/composition/train_aesthetic.py --data pairs.jsonl --out
   aesthetic_head.pt`, export the checkpoint, then write held-out winner/loser scores into
   the schema printed by `python ml/models/composition/eval_composition.py --schema` and
   run `--evaluate results.json`. The gate is a *comparison*: **a learned ranker that
   cannot beat the twelve-term linear reference model has learned nothing worth shipping.**
   That comparison is why the reference model outlives the placeholder.

## Quality gate

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aesthetic pairwise agreement | >= 0.78 | `fixtures::composition_pairs`, through the reference model | **met (reference model only)** |
| Taste cannot override geometric substance | exact | `fixtures::beautiful_but_cut` | **met** |
| Out-of-focus beauty loses in Phase 12 | exact | Phase 12 integration | **deferred (C8): Phase 12 does not exist yet** |
| Scene conditioning changes the answer | exact | one frame scored in two scenes | **met** |
| Learned head beats the reference model | - | - | **deferred (C1, C2): needs photographer pairs** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

The second row is the one that matters most and it is a *structural* gate rather than a
statistical one: a frame with a beautiful arrangement and a cut neck must lose to a plain
frame with nothing wrong, at every value of `aesthetic_weight` in every rule row. It is met
because of the cap, not because of the model.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained, and the reference model that stands in for it has four
   coefficients set by argument.** Both are exit-report conditions.
2. **It encodes one taste.** Section 12's second failure mode. The mitigations shipped are
   the scene conditioning and the cap; the mitigation not shipped is multi-photographer
   labels, which needs data.
3. **Garbage in.** The head reads twelve measurements and cannot tell a wrong one from a
   right one. A wedding at 4 % `keypoint_aware` feeds it a `HeadMerge` of zero and a
   headroom of zero on nearly every frame, and it will score those frames confidently.
   `CompositionOutline::keypoint_aware` is how a caller finds that out.
4. **The output is not a percentage.** 0.62 does not mean "62 % well composed". It is a
   ranker's score and only differences within a scene are meaningful. The manifest says
   `ranker` for this reason and the panel never shows the raw number.
5. **int8 is permitted here and forbidden on `pose_keypoints`.** The cap bounds what this
   number can do to a composite at a quarter in either direction, so a quantisation error
   of a few thousandths moves a composition score by less than a thousandth.

## Fallback

Complete, and it is visible rather than silent. `Analyser::taste` falls back to
`Aesthetic::from_features`, and the fallback is announced three ways: `Aesthetic::learned`
is false, the judgement carries `CompositionCode::AestheticUnavailable` with its own
sentence in the panel, and the confidence drops by 0.06. A caller must be able to tell "the
head said 0.5" from "there was no head and geometry alone said 0.5".

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets a full framing judgement, a full set of
flags and a composition score on every frame. What it loses is the part of the score that
is taste, which is at most a quarter of it by construction.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_composition` row stale, so the next pass re-judges; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5043`).
