## Purpose

Read what a face is doing, as eight numbers that do not compete.

PHASE-10 section 2.1 is explicit that the outputs are "all continuous, not one-hot", and
that requirement is the whole design of this head rather than a detail of it. A wedding is
full of faces that are laughing and crying at the same time; a mother at a first look is
tender, surprised and wet-eyed in one frame. A softmax would make the eight channels
compete for one unit of probability and force the model to choose between them - and a
model forced to choose produces exactly the technically-perfect, emotionally-flat gallery
that section 1 names as "the most common complaint about AI culling today".

So the activation is a `Sigmoid`, it is in the graph rather than in the decoder, and
`xtask models` records the task as `multilabel` rather than `multiclass` so that nobody
reading the manifest has to infer it.

**Two of the eight channels carry more weight than the other six**, in opposite
directions:

* **`composed`** is a *positive* class. Section 6.1: "composure is a positive class: in
  `ritual` and `vows` scenes, `composed` with mutual gaze can outscore a smile." A head
  that learned composure as the absence of expression would make
  `emotion_weights.toml`'s cultural correction impossible to apply, because there would be
  nothing to weight.
* **`tears`** is the one channel a false positive is embarrassing on. Section 12's fourth
  failure mode names it, `TEARS_CERTAIN` is 0.85, and no reason text in the product says
  the word below that.

## Architecture

A small convolutional stem into eight independent sigmoids:

```
crop [N, 3, 112, 112]
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (24ch) -> Relu          # 112 -> 56
  -> MaxPool 2x2                          # 56 -> 28
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (48ch) -> Relu          # 28 -> 14
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 48x48 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 8x48 -> Sigmoid -> expression
```

Three decisions in it are deliberate.

**Three-channel input.** Unlike phase 09's focus head, which is single-channel because
focus is a luminance question. The channel most likely to be wrong without colour is
`tears`: it is a specular wet cue on the lower lid, and what separates a tear track from a
highlight on cheekbone makeup is its colour rather than its shape.

**112 px, and the same crop as the recogniser's.** `aura_vision::face::align` already
produces exactly this crop once per face for phase 06 and again for phase 09's eye head.
Asking for a different size would mean a second warp per face for no gain, and this is the
third consumer of that geometry - which is what makes running phases 06, 09 and 10 back to
back nearly free.

**No gaze slot.** Where a face is looking is *geometry* - phase 06's two eye landmarks
against the face box - so it is measured in `aura_brain_wedding::emotion::gaze` rather
than predicted. A model that appeared to answer it would be guessing with authority about
something that can be computed, and it would be one more output to retrain when the
vocabulary changes.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::expression_head`. On a real face crop it produces eight
sigmoids that describe a random projection of the pixels.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-10-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger**, shared
with `interaction_head`.

The reason is the one phases 05 to 09 all give, with one addition specific to this head:
there is no labelled wedding data in this repository, no consented dataset and no GPU
backend - and section 9's `DATA` deliverable is "expression labels, interaction boxes,
**10k pairwise photographer comparisons across traditions**", which is nine days of work
by a person who has access to weddings and to photographers.

What the day a real dataset arrives looks like:

1. `ml/models/emotion/train_expression.py --build-dataset` samples aligned crops from the
   catalog at the same 112 px the analyser uses, **stratified by tradition and by scene**.
   Not by expression: a uniform sample of a Western dataset would be 70 % smiles and would
   teach the head that composure is what a face does when nothing is happening.
2. **Wedding-level splits, never frame-level.** Two frames from the same burst three
   hundred milliseconds apart are near-duplicates; a frame-level split puts one in train
   and one in test and reports an accuracy that measures leakage. Phase 09's card says the
   same thing and the mistake is easier to make here, because a moment has more frames.
3. Label from three sources rather than one: a photographer's own keep/reject gives a weak
   signal on the whole face, an expression annotator gives the eight channels, and the
   **pairwise comparisons** give the ranker its targets. The three are not the same task
   and must not be collected in one pass.
4. **The `tears` channel needs deliberate mining and a precision-weighted loss.** Random
   sampling produces almost none of it, and the operating point the product needs is
   0.90 precision at whatever recall that costs.
5. **Per-tradition metrics are mandatory, not optional.** `eval_emotion.py --per-tradition`
   reports the eight channels' agreement separately for each tradition in the set, and a
   head whose composure agreement is 0.9 on Christian weddings and 0.6 on Hindu ones has
   not been trained - it has been trained on the wrong thing. That number is condition C5
   of phase 07's exit report in a different clothing, and it is the number this phase would
   be judged on.

The arithmetic that makes those plausible: 13,832 parameters, with the convolutions
dominating at about 20 MFLOP for a 112 px crop. Three faces is 60 MFLOP, which is a few
milliseconds on the pure-Rust interpreter. **The per-frame budget is dominated by the
interaction head's 160 px four-plane resample and by one warp per face, not by this.**

## Quality gate

Section 10.1's expression thresholds, and what each is measured against today.

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Painted expression survives the warp | within 0.05 | `fixtures::EmotionFrame`, five profiles | **met** |
| Tears F1 | >= 0.85 | painted ground truth | **met** (1.000) |
| Tears precision | >= 0.90 | painted ground truth | **met** (1.000) |
| Laughter F1 | >= 0.85 | painted ground truth | **met** (1.000) |
| No tear text below `TEARS_CERTAIN` | exact | `fixtures::crying` at 0.75 and 0.94 | **met** |
| Composure fairness in ritual scenes | not systematically lower | `fixtures::composed` against `fixtures::smiling` | **met**, 3 of 3 rites |
| Pairwise agreement | >= 0.80 | eight authored comparisons | **met** (1.000), but see C2 |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two reads of one frame | **met** |

Read the "measured against" column. Every row marked met is a real measurement of the
*pipeline* - the warp, the reference reader, the prominence weighting, the nine features,
the scene table, the tradition multipliers, the ranker and the certainty gate - against
ground truth painted into the pixels. **None of them is a measurement of this model's
weights.**

`the_gate_rejects_a_useless_reader` in `tests/eval/emotion_eval.rs` is the guard: a reader
that returns the same eight numbers for every face - which is very nearly what an untrained
head does - fails the agreement gate rather than passing it.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and applies
   equally to a trained version.
2. **A face with no landmarks is skipped, not guessed at.** `expression::crop_for` returns
   `None` and the face gets a `FaceExpression::unread` row. A crop built from collapsed
   landmarks is a rectangle of somebody's shoulder, and eight confident readings about a
   shoulder is worse than no reading at all.
3. **112 px is a real ceiling.** A face 40 px tall in the proxy upsamples into the crop
   and the head sees an interpolation. Phase 06's quality gate already excludes such faces
   from voting on identity; here they carry less weight in the prominence mean, because
   `prominence_weighted` multiplies by the head's own confidence.
4. **`genuineness` is the channel most at risk of encoding a cultural norm.** The Duchenne
   cue is real and it is not uniformly expressed; a head trained on one tradition's smiles
   will call another's posed. The mitigation is structural rather than in the weights:
   `[scene.family_portrait]` weights genuineness at 0.15, and feature 1 multiplies it by
   `smile` so it can only ever damp a term rather than penalise a face.
5. **`composed` and "no expression" are genuinely hard to separate**, trained or not, and
   the difference matters more here than anywhere in the product. A trained version is
   expected to confuse them most in low light, which is where Hindu ceremonies are shot.
6. **A re-render invalidates every reading.** The head reads phase 02's documented proxy;
   a `PIPELINE_VER` bump changes the pixels it sees. `image_interaction.model_ver` and the
   proxy's `PIPELINE_VER` are separate columns in separate tables, and `AURA-ML-5038` is
   what stops two vintages being compared.
7. **int8 is forbidden here and permitted on `interaction_head`.** Eight independent
   sigmoids quantised per tensor lose most of their resolution near 0 and 1, and one of the
   eight is read against a 0.85 threshold that decides whether the product says the word
   "crying" and whether phase 09 exonerates a closed eye.

## Fallback

Complete, and it has two layers.

1. **Per frame**: a head that raises is caught by `EmotionPass::run_ids`, coded
   `AURA-ML-5040`, and **no row is written** - so the frame is retried next pass and is
   never stored as "nothing happening in this photograph".
2. **Per reading**: with the head uninstalled, every frame is read on the interaction head
   alone. `EmotionCode::NoFaces` goes into the reasons, the confidence is capped at 0.55,
   and `EmotionOutline::face_aware` reports zero - so a caller about to trust an ordering
   can see that seven of the nine ranker terms were absent.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets an emotion score on every frame, and the
product says loudly what that score is missing.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_interaction` row stale, so the next pass re-reads; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5038`), and a photographer's peak choices and pairwise preferences survive
untouched - they are in `moment_peak.user_chosen` and `emotion_preferences`, and neither
is derived from a model.
