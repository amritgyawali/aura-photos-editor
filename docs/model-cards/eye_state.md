## Purpose

Say what a pair of eyes is doing, so that the rules in
`crates/aura-brain-photo/src/integrity/eyes.rs` can decide whether it matters.

This is the trust test of the whole product. PHASE-09 section 1:

> Blink handling is the classic trust test. Closed eyes during a kiss, a prayer, a
> first-look reaction or a tearful hug are *good* photographs. A naive eye-open detector
> destroys exactly the frames that sell weddings.

The head's job is deliberately narrow. It answers **what the eyes are doing** and nothing
else. Whether that is a defect depends on the scene, on who the person is, and on what the
other partner's eyes are doing - none of which is in the crop.

### The five classes, and why there are five

| Slot | Class | Why it is its own class |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `open` | the default, and the class the head must be conservative about leaving |
| 1 | `squint` | sun, laughter, a flash about to fire - a mild defect, not a blink |
| 2 | `closed` | the only class that can raise `EYES_CLOSED` |
| 3 | `looking_down` | **looks like `closed` to a classifier and means the opposite to a photographer** |
| 4 | `occluded` | a veil, a hand, a glass, another head - unknown, not shut |

Slots 3 and 4 are the reason this is a model rather than an eyelid-aperture heuristic.
Open against closed is nearly separable geometrically. Open against *looking down* is not,
and a bride looking down at her bouquet is a photograph while a bride mid-blink is a
reshoot. Reading `occluded` as `closed` turns every veiled face in a Hindu ceremony into a
defect.

**The slot order is a contract.** It is `EyeOpenness::ALL`, the contract says so, this card
repeats it, and renumbering that enum relabels a trained model.

## Architecture

```
crop [N, 3, 112, 112]
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (16ch) -> Relu          # 112 -> 56
  -> MaxPool 2x2                          # 56 -> 28
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (48ch) -> Relu          # 28 -> 14
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 48x48 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 5x48 -> Softmax -> eye_probs
```

Three-channel, unlike `focus_head`, and the reason is slot 4: an eye behind a veil or a
pair of sunglasses is an occlusion, and colour is most of what separates it from a closed
eyelid.

The softmax is in the graph by the rule `face_quality` states - an activation belongs in a
graph when it applies to every channel of a tensor - and the five classes are mutually
exclusive, so it qualifies.

**No "intentional" slot.** Every one of the four intent rules depends on something outside
the crop. A model given the crop alone and asked "was this deliberate" would be guessing
with authority, and section 9 puts the rules in front of a PM and a working photographer
for sign-off precisely because they are a *policy* rather than a measurement.

### The crop it reads

A two-point similarity warp from the two eye landmarks onto the canonical `ArcFace`
positions - `aura_brain_photo::integrity::eyes::align_from_eyes`. Not phase 06's five-point
least-squares fit: `FaceRef` carries two eye points and not the nose and mouth corners,
because those are the half of the landmark set that makes a face identifiable and
ADR-0019 section 3 keeps them out.

Two points determine a similarity transform exactly, which is all the normalisation an
eye-state question needs: it puts the eyes on a horizontal line at a known separation, so a
head tilted into a hug is comparable with a head held level. What is lost is robustness to
one mis-detected landmark, and the mitigation is that a face with no usable landmarks is
**skipped rather than cropped from its box** - `FaceRef::has_eyes` - because a box crop of
a tilted head puts the eyes on a diagonal and the head would answer about the wrong pixels.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::eye_state`. On a real face it produces a distribution over
five classes that describes a random projection of the crop.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-09-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger**, shared
with `focus_head`.

Two reasons, and the second is the harder one. There is no consented face data in this
repository - phase 06's condition C1, unchanged - and eye-state labelling is *more*
sensitive than detection labelling rather than less: a labelled set of closed-eye
photographs of identifiable people at private events is exactly the kind of dataset
`docs/adr/ADR-0013` and `docs/pdf/24-PRIVACY-CONSENT-AND-LEGAL` exist to govern.

What the day a consented dataset arrives looks like:

1. `ml/models/integrity/train_eyes.py --build-dataset` reads aligned crops through
   `PeopleService`'s sealed store, under the project's consent record, and never writes a
   crop outside it.
2. **Label the five classes, and label the intent separately.** Section 8 step 7 says
   "label eye states including intentional-closed cases", and the two labels are
   independent: a frame is `closed` *and* `intentional`, and conflating them into a
   four-class problem is how a model ends up predicting intent from a crop.
3. **The class balance is brutal** - `open` is 90 %+ of any real sample. Class-balanced
   sampling, and evaluate with F1 rather than accuracy, which section 10.1 already asks
   for.
4. `looking_down` needs deliberate mining from `getting_ready`, `details` and `speeches`,
   where people look at what they are holding.
5. `eval_integrity.py --blink-f1` measures section 10.1's F1 and the intentional-closed
   false-positive rate on a held-out set, and the gate in `tests/eval/integrity_eval.rs`
   is re-pointed from `fixtures::ReferenceEyeReader` at real crops.

## Quality gate

Section 10.1's eye thresholds, and what each is measured against today.

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blink F1 | >= 0.95 | `fixtures::ReferenceEyeReader` + labelled markers | **algorithm passes; weights not measured (C1)** |
| Intentional-closed false positives | <= 2 % | the kiss/vows/hug fixture set | **algorithm passes; weights not measured (C1)** |
| Group closed-eye ratio matches a human count | exact | `fixtures::group_frame` | **met** |
| Only important identities' eyes gate a frame | exact | constructed prominence | **met** |
| A guest blinking in a crowd is not a defect | exact | `fixtures::group_frame` at low prominence | **met** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

**Read the first two rows carefully.** `ReferenceEyeReader` replaces the head with a known
answer decoded from a marker painted into the fixture. What that measures is everything
*around* the head - the alignment, the gating thresholds, the four intent rules, the group
ratio and the flag decision - and it is a real result about those. It says nothing whatever
about the shipped weights. Phase 06 drew this line with its `ReferenceDetector`; this is the
same line.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and applies
   equally to a trained version.
2. **Sunglasses are `occluded` and should be.** A trained head that learned to call them
   `closed` would flag every outdoor exit frame in a summer wedding.
3. **A face with no landmarks is skipped.** No eye state is stored, the face does not gate,
   and the frame is scored without it. Silent in the sense that no error is raised - it is
   a legitimate outcome, not a failure - but visible in `IntegrityResult::gating_faces`
   being lower than the face count.
4. **An uncertain `closed` never raises a flag.** `eyes::ACT_ON_CLOSED` is 0.55, and below
   it the state is stored with its confidence and does not gate. This is one-directional
   on purpose: an uncertain *open* needs no threshold because it costs nothing.
5. **The tears rule is not implemented.** Section 6.4's third intent rule needs phase 10.
   `IntentInput::tears` is wired through and always false, which makes a tearful closed-eye
   frame *look* more defective than it is - the safe direction, visible in a review queue.
   Condition C4.
6. **int8 is forbidden.** This head can convict a photograph, and the decision lives at
   0.55 where per-tensor int8 has least resolution. Contrast with `focus_head`, where int8
   is permitted precisely because that head can only exonerate.

## Fairness

**Not published, and not approximated.**

Phase 06's condition C5 applies with a specific shape here. Eye-state classification is
known in the literature to vary with epicanthic fold morphology, eyelid aperture and eye
colour, all of which correlate with ethnicity - and a head that is worse at "open" for one
group would systematically mark that group's photographs as defective. **That is the single
most direct route from this product to a discriminatory outcome**, and it is why no number
is published without the analysis.

What is needed before one is:

- per-group F1 on a consented, balanced test set, with the groups agreed with PM rather
  than chosen by whoever built the set;
- the same figures at low light and small face sizes, where a disparity appears first;
- an agreed maximum disparity, decided in advance.

Two mitigations are already implemented rather than promised: the gating thresholds mean
most faces in a frame are never judged at all, and `ACT_ON_CLOSED` means an uncertain
reading raises nothing. Both reduce the exposure; neither substitutes for the measurement.

## Fallback

Complete, and it has three layers.

1. **Per face**: a face with no usable landmarks is skipped, with no eye state and no gate.
2. **Per frame**: a head that raises is caught by `IntegrityPass::run_ids`, coded
   `AURA-ML-5035`, and **no row is written** - so the frame is retried and is never stored
   as "nobody was blinking".
3. **Per wedding**: with the head uninstalled, no `face_eye_state` rows exist,
   `closed_eye_ratio` is 0.0 with `gating_faces` 0 - which is distinguishable from "nobody
   blinked", by construction - and the eye sub-score is 1.0. Every other verdict stands.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets focus, motion, exposure and noise on every
frame.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_integrity` and `face_eye_state` row stale, so the next pass re-analyses; two
versions are never compared (`AURA-ML-5033`).
