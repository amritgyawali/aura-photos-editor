## Purpose

Say what one photograph is of, so that no threshold in the rest of the product has to be
global.

Invariant 7 - "no threshold is global; every threshold is a function of the detected
scene and subject role" - has been an unenforceable promise since phase 01, because there
was nothing to condition on. This model produces the condition. Phase 09 asks how noisy a
frame may be, phase 12 asks how many of a kind to keep, phases 15 to 17 ask how it should
be graded, and all three of those questions are answered by
`crates/aura-brain-wedding/config/scene_profiles.toml` indexed by this model's output.

The fourteen attributes are a second, independent answer to a different question: not
*what* the photograph is but *what it is like*. Indoors, flash, backlit, night, tungsten,
crowd, stage, vehicle, food, decor, kids, pets, rain. They are sigmoids rather than a
second softmax because a frame can be indoors *and* backlit *and* have a crowd, and a
mutually exclusive encoding would force the model to choose between three true things.

## Architecture

An **adapter**, not a backbone. This is the whole of PHASE-07 section 6.1:

> Shared trunk = the Phase 05 embedding (frozen) plus a small trainable adapter, so scene
> inference costs almost nothing extra per image.

So the model takes no pixels. It takes the 512-d vector `wedding_embedding` already
produced for every photograph in the project, concatenated with sixteen numbers the
catalog already holds, and runs them through one 256-wide hidden layer to two heads:

```
features [N, 528] -> Gemm 256x528 -> Relu -> +-> Gemm 22x256 -> Softmax -> scene_probs
                                             |
                                             +-> Gemm 14x256 -> Sigmoid -> attr_probs
```

Both activations are in the graph, by the rule `face_quality` states: an activation
belongs in a graph when it applies to every channel of a tensor and in the decoder when it
does not. A softmax over all 22 classes and a sigmoid over all 14 attributes both qualify.

**The abstention is not in the graph.** `SceneId::Unknown` is a decision
`scene::classifier::decode_scene` makes from the top-1 margin, and the reason is that a
model cannot usefully be trained to say "I am not sure" through an output slot that
competes with the classes it is unsure between: the gradient that teaches it to pick
`unknown` is the same gradient that teaches it not to pick `ceremony`, and those are not
the same lesson.

### The sixteen context features

Section 6.1's list, in the order the trained head will expect them.
`crates/aura-brain-wedding/src/scene/attributes.rs` is the implementation and
`ml/models/scene/dataset.py` repeats the numbering, because the order is a contract and
reordering it silently relabels every stored posterior.

| Slots | Feature | Neutral when unknown |
|---|---|---|
| 0, 1 | hour of the wedding, as a sine and a cosine over 24 h | (0, 0) - the centre of the circle, which is not any hour |
| 2 | fraction of a day since the first frame, saturating | 0 |
| 3 | flash fired, from EXIF | 0.5 |
| 4-7 | ISO bucket, one-hot: base / 800 / 3200 / above | all zero |
| 8 | face count from phase 06, saturating at twelve | 0 |
| 9 | one of the couple is present, from `PeopleService` | 0.5 |
| 10-13 | luminance shape: p50, p1, p99, total clipping | all zero |
| 14, 15 | colour temperature, normalised, plus a present flag | (0, 0) |

Two slots for the hour rather than one, because a wedding that runs past midnight is the
common case for three of the five traditions in the shipped taxonomy, and a linear feature
would put the vidaai at the opposite end of the day from the saptapadi half an hour before
it.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::scene_classifier`. Run on a real photograph it produces a
posterior that describes a random projection of the embedding, not a wedding.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-07-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger.**

The reason is the same one phases 05 and 06 give and it has not moved: there is no
labelled wedding data in this repository, no consented dataset and no GPU backend. Section
8 step 2 of the phase document asks for "the fixture weddings and an expansion set
covering four traditions (DATA)" and that work has not been done.

What the day a real dataset arrives looks like:

1. `ml/models/scene/dataset.py --build` assembles per-frame examples from the catalog:
   the stored embedding, the sixteen context features, and a label.
2. Wedding-level splits, never frame-level. Two frames from the same ceremony three
   seconds apart are near-duplicates, and a frame-level split puts one in train and one in
   test and reports an accuracy that is a measurement of leakage.
3. `train_multihead.py` with focal loss on the scene head - section 6.1, because `kiss`
   and `first_look` have two orders of magnitude fewer examples than `candid` - and plain
   BCE on the attributes.
4. `eval_scene.py --calibrate` produces the per-attribute thresholds that replace the
   single `ATTRIBUTE_THRESHOLD = 0.5` this build uses.
5. The gates in `tests/eval/scene_eval.rs` are re-pointed from the synthetic fixtures at
   the real test set, and section 10.1's numbers become claims about photographs.

Left empty rather than filled with plausible figures, exactly as phases 03 and 06 leave
their reference-machine rows empty. `crates/aura-perf/tests/scene_budgets.rs` asserts the
budget that matters - section 11's 35 seconds for four thousand images - on whatever
machine CI runs on, and the reference-machine table is filled in when a reference machine
exists.

The arithmetic that makes 35 s plausible: 144,676 parameters is one 528x256 matrix
multiply plus two small ones, about 300 kFLOP per frame. Four thousand frames is 1.2
GFLOP, which is under a second of arithmetic. **The budget is dominated by reading four
thousand embeddings out of SQLite, not by this model**, which is why
`scene::pass::BATCH` is about the write transaction rather than about the tensor.

## Quality gate

Section 10.1's thresholds, and what each is measured against today.

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene top-1 accuracy | >= 0.92 | synthetic posteriors at a driven 0.94 accuracy | **algorithm passes; weights not measured (C1)** |
| Per-class accuracy, classes with > 200 samples | >= 0.85 | - | **deferred (C2): needs a labelled set** |
| Abstention rules do what they claim | - | constructed distributions | **met** |
| Attribute measurement overrides the head | - | constructed EXIF | **met** |
| A useless classifier fails the gate | - | a flat distribution | **met** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

Read the "measured against" column. Every number in it is a real measurement of the
*algorithms* - the softmax decode, the top-3 ordering, the margin rule, the attribute
correction - on ground truth whose answer is known by construction. None of them is a
measurement of the shipped weights.

`the_gate_rejects_a_useless_classifier` exists so that this is checkable rather than
asserted: it proves the harness would fail a model that had learned nothing.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and applies
   equally to a trained version.
2. **A wedding with no embeddings has no scenes.** The trunk is frozen and upstream; a
   project half-way through its phase 05 pass gets scenes for half its frames and
   `StoryOutline::coverage` says so. This is a coverage report, not a failure.
3. **The two `getting_ready` classes are a question about people, not about pixels.** A
   trained head will confuse `getting_ready_bride` and `getting_ready_groom` at a rate the
   embedding cannot fix, because the difference is who is in the room. `PeopleService` is
   the right answer to that and the context features carry a hint of it (slot 9), but the
   confusion is structural and is in the fixture's confusion table for that reason.
4. **Attributes that are also EXIF are decided, not predicted.** `flash`, `night`,
   `tungsten` and `indoor` are overridden by measurements where measurements exist. This
   stays correct after training: a trained head will still be wrong about flash on a frame
   lit by a window at 1/200th, and the camera will not.
5. **A re-embed invalidates every posterior.** The head is an adapter on a specific trunk.
   `image_scenes.embed_ver` records which trunk was read, and a mismatch is
   `AURA-ML-5022`, never a silent comparison.
6. **int8 is forbidden.** A 22-way softmax quantised per tensor loses most of its
   resolution exactly at the top-1 margin, which is where the abstention decision is made
   and where every downstream threshold hangs.

## Fairness

**Not published, and not approximated.**

Phase 06's condition C5 applies here with a different shape. A scene classifier does not
recognise people, but it is not neutral either: `getting_ready`, `ritual` and `ceremony`
look different across traditions, and a head trained mostly on Western weddings will read
a mandap as `stage` + `crowd` + `other` and a nikah as `speeches`. That is a *cultural*
disparity rather than a demographic one, and it is exactly the moat section 1 claims.

What is needed before a number is published:

- per-tradition top-1 accuracy, on a test set with balanced coverage of the five shipped
  traditions, and per-tradition **recall on the pivotal scenes** (`SceneId::is_pivotal`)
  rather than overall accuracy, which hides the failure;
- the same figures on the night and mixed-light subsets, where a disparity appears first;
- an agreed maximum disparity, decided with PM rather than reported without a threshold.

The mitigation is already implemented rather than promised: where the evidence is weak the
decoder abstains - `SceneId::Unknown` with the top-3 intact - which turns a cultural
failure into a visible gap and a `needs_review` flag rather than a confident wrong label
in a client's timeline.

## Fallback

Complete, and it has three layers.

1. **Per frame**: a batch that fails is retried one frame at a time; a frame that still
   fails is `AURA-ML-5027`, no row is written, and the next pass retries it.
2. **Per label**: below the confidence floor or the margin the frame is
   `SceneId::Unknown` with its top-3 preserved, so the HMM can still use the distribution
   and the Explain panel can still show what the head thought.
3. **Per wedding**: with no scenes at all, segmentation falls back to time gaps
   (`AURA-ML-5026`), the chapters are flagged for review, and every later phase reads
   `StoryOutline::coverage` and takes its own non-scene path.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still imports, embeds, culls and delivers.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_scenes` row stale, so the next pass re-classifies; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5022`).
