## Purpose

Answer what is happening *between* people, which no per-face model can see.

`expression_head` reads one face at a time and would never know that two of them are
kissing. PHASE-10 section 2.1 lists nine interactions - hug, kiss, hand-hold, dance-hold,
ring exchange, blessing, toast, tears being wiped, group cheer - and four of them
(`Interaction::is_milestone`) are the frames a client buys a print of regardless of how
the faces read.

**Multi-label, not multi-class.** A frame can be a hug *and* tears being wiped, and it very
often is: somebody wiping somebody else's tears is holding them. Section 5 spells the field
`Vec<(Interaction, f32)>` for that reason, and the activation is a `Sigmoid` in the graph
rather than a `Softmax`. `xtask models` records the task as `multilabel` so that nobody
reading the manifest has to infer it.

## Architecture

Three strided convolution blocks into nine independent sigmoids:

```
frame [N, 4, 160, 160]
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (16ch) -> Relu          # 160 -> 80
  -> MaxPool 2x2                          # 80 -> 40
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (32ch) -> Relu          # 40 -> 20
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (48ch) -> Relu          # 20 -> 10
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 48x48 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 9x48 -> Sigmoid -> interaction_probs
```

Three decisions in it are deliberate.

**The person prior is a fourth plane, not appended coordinates.** Section 6.2 says the head
"operates on the full frame with person boxes as spatial priors". There are two ways to
give a convolutional network that prior - append the box coordinates to the flattened
feature vector, or paint them into an input channel - and only the second is a prior in any
useful sense. Coordinates appended after the global pool arrive *after* every spatial
decision has been made; a plane arrives at the first convolution, so the network can learn
"two prior blobs overlapping, low in the frame" as one feature, which is what a hug looks
like. It costs a third of the stem's arithmetic.

The plane is painted from phase 06's face boxes grown downwards into torsos -
`TORSO_HEIGHTS` is four box heights and `SHOULDER_WIDTHS` is one either side. A frame with
no face pass gets an all-zero plane, which is a real input meaning "nobody was located"
rather than a missing one, and `interaction::confidence` scales the answer down by 0.70
when that happens.

**160 px, not 224.** An interaction is a *pose* question - are two bodies in contact, is an
arm raised, are two heads close - and pose survives downsampling far better than focus
does. At 224 px the head costs three times as much for a distinction nobody has been able
to name, and section 11 budgets 40 ms for the whole per-frame pipeline.

**One extra convolution block over `expression_head`.** The receptive field has to reach
across two people rather than across one face. That is what the third stride buys, and it
is why the per-frame cost is dominated by this head rather than by the batched one.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::interaction_head`. On a real frame it produces nine sigmoids
that describe a random projection of the pixels.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-10-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger**, shared
with `expression_head`.

Section 9 gives `DATA` "interaction boxes" as part of a nine-day deliverable and section 8
step 4 says "train the interaction head with person-box priors". Neither has been done,
for the reason every phase since 05 gives: there is no labelled wedding data here.

What the day a real dataset arrives looks like:

1. `ml/models/emotion/train_interaction.py --build-dataset` samples whole frames at 160 px
   with the prior plane rendered from the *stored* face boxes, so that training and
   inference see the same prior. A prior rendered from ground-truth boxes at training time
   and from a detector's boxes at inference time is a train/serve skew that looks like a
   modelling failure.
2. **Label at the frame level with a multi-hot vector**, not with boxes around the
   interaction. The head is asked what is happening, not where; a localisation target
   would be a different and more expensive annotation task for an output the product does
   not have a field for.
3. **The nine classes are wildly unbalanced.** `hand_hold` and `hug` are in a tenth of a
   wedding's frames; `ring_exchange` is in twenty. Class-balanced sampling, and a
   per-class operating point rather than one `DETECTION_FLOOR` for all nine, is the first
   thing a trained version should change about this file.
4. **`blessing` is the class most at risk of a cultural failure**, and it is the reason
   this head has a `blessing` class at all rather than folding rituals into `hug`. A
   tilak, feet being touched, a hand on a head and a priest's blessing are one class in the
   vocabulary and four quite different images; a training set drawn from one tradition
   would learn one of them.
5. `eval_emotion.py --per-class` reports precision and recall for each of the nine
   separately, and a head whose macro-F1 is respectable because `hug` is excellent and
   `ring_exchange` is at chance has not been trained for this product.

this is a whole-wedding cost of about two minutes rather than of eight.

## Quality gate

Section 10.1 sets no interaction-specific gate; interactions feed feature 2 of nine and are
measured through the ranker. What is gated today:

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A detected milestone raises a frame's score | strict | `fixtures::interaction_vector(Kiss, 0.9)` in `SceneId::Kiss` | **met** |
| Ties resolve in the head's output order | exact | two channels at 0.80 | **met** |
| A weak channel does not reach the stored row | exact | `DETECTION_FLOOR`, nine channels at 0.05 | **met** |
| A kiss scene plus a kiss interaction is a kiss apex | exact | `peak::kind_of` | **met** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two reads of one frame | **met** |

Every row is a measurement of the *plumbing* - the floor, the ordering, the milestone
bonus, the peak-kind derivation - against a planted vector. **None is a measurement of this
model's weights**, and there is no synthetic frame in this repository that contains a real
hug.

That last sentence is worth sitting with. `expression_head`'s gates are measured against an
answer painted into the pixels and read back through the real warp, which is a genuine test
of everything except the weights. This head has no equivalent trick: its input is the whole
frame, and painting a marker into it would test nothing. So its answer is planted in every
gate, and the exit report says so where the numbers are reported.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and applies
   equally to a trained version.
2. **No person prior means a reading on colour alone.** A wedding with no face pass gets an
   all-zero fourth plane. The head still answers - two people embracing look like two
   people embracing - but the spatial evidence section 6.2 asks for is absent, and
   `NO_PRIOR_CONFIDENCE_SCALE` cuts the confidence to 0.70 of what it would be.
3. **The prior is a torso approximation, not a body box.** `FaceRef` carries only the face
   box, so the prior is a face box grown downwards. It errs wide on purpose: a prior
   slightly too large blurs the boundary between two people, and a prior too narrow says
   two people who are touching are not.
4. **160 px loses a ring.** `ring_exchange` is the smallest thing in the vocabulary and it
   is the class most likely to be missed at this resolution. The mitigation is that the
   scene carries it - `[scene.rings]` weights the interaction term at 1.45, the highest in
   the table - so a missed detection costs a frame less than it would elsewhere.
5. **A crowd looks like a group cheer.** `group_cheer` is the class most likely to fire on
   a busy reception where nothing is happening, and it is the one whose false positives
   would inflate the emotion score of a whole chapter. `DETECTION_FLOOR` at 0.35 and the
   milestone list excluding it are both there for that.
6. **int8 is permitted here and forbidden on `expression_head`.** Nine coarse pose
   questions over a 160 px frame are decided far from any threshold that matters - an
   interaction at 0.31 and one at 0.34 both read as "weakly present" and feed one of nine
   ranker terms. Nothing here can convict a photograph, and this head runs once on every
   frame in the wedding.

## Fallback

Complete, and it has two layers.

1. **Per frame**: a head that raises is caught by `EmotionPass::run_ids`, coded
   `AURA-ML-5040`, and **no row is written**.
2. **Per reading**: with the head uninstalled, every frame is read on the faces alone. The
   interactions list is empty, feature 2 is zero, and the score falls by whatever that
   term was worth in the scene. A detail shot with no faces and no head gets its bias and
   `EmotionCode::NoFaces` - which is an honest 0.1 rather than a missing row.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_interaction` row stale, so the next pass re-reads. Because the version integer is
shared with `expression_head`, rolling one back rolls both back - which is deliberate: they
ship, are validated and are compared as one pack, and a catalog holding faces from one
vintage and interactions from another is a state nothing in the product could interpret.
