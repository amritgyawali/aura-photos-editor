## Purpose

Say where a person's joints are, so that [`crop_audit`] can say what the frame edge cut.

PHASE-11 section 2.1 asks for "crop violation detection using body keypoints: cut at
joints (wrist, elbow, knee, ankle), top-of-head crop, half-limb crop". Every one of those
is a question about a *coordinate against a frame edge*, and none of them is answerable
from a bounding box. A box tells you a person is partly out of shot; it cannot tell you
whether the edge fell on their wrist - which is the difference between a photograph that
reads as tightly cropped and one that reads as a severed hand.

## Architecture

A four-stage convolutional stem into a coordinate vector:

```
person [N, 3, 192, 192]
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (24ch) -> Relu           # 192 -> 96
  -> MaxPool 2x2                           # 96  -> 48
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (48ch) -> Relu           # 48  -> 24
  -> Conv 3x3 s2 (64ch) -> Relu           # 24  -> 12
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 96x64 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 51x96 -> Sigmoid -> keypoints
```

Three decisions in it are deliberate, and the first is the one a reader should argue with.

**A coordinate vector, not heatmaps.** Every serious pose model emits `[N, 17, H, W]`
heatmaps and decodes them with an argmax plus a sub-pixel refinement. That is more
accurate, and it is 39,000 floats per person instead of 51. The deterministic interpreter
phase 03 ships would spend most of a wedding's compute budget on that decode, and section
11 budgets **30 ms for the whole composition analysis** - the tightest per-image budget in
the wedding brain. The decoder in `composition::keypoints::decode` reads a vector, so
swapping in a heatmap model later is a change to that one function and to nothing else.

**192 px, larger than any other crop in the product.** Faces are analysed at 112 px by
three separate heads. A wrist inside a full-length person crop at 112 px is two pixels
across, and section 10.1 asks for a limb-crop F1 of 0.90 - a number that is not reachable
from a crop where the joint being tested is smaller than the localisation error.

**Colour, not luminance.** The focus head reads one channel because focus is a luminance
question. This is not: what separates an arm from the wall behind it in a dark reception
is usually its colour.

### The coordinates are crop-relative, and may be negative

The head emits `0..1` inside the person crop. `decode` maps each point back through the
crop's placement in the frame, and the crop is a *derived* box that routinely extends past
the frame edge - so a decoded `x` below zero is a real answer meaning "this joint is off
the left of the photograph". That is the whole point: a joint that is off-frame is what a
cut is.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::pose_keypoints`. On a real person crop it produces seventeen
coordinates that are a random projection of that crop.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-11-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger**, shared
with `aesthetic_head`.

The reason is the one every model since phase 05 gives: there is no labelled wedding data
in this repository and no GPU backend. Unlike the face models, this one has an unusually
clear path out - COCO keypoints is a public dataset with 250,000 labelled people in it,
and a pose model is the least wedding-specific model in the product. Section 9 gives SRML
"keypoint model integration" precisely because the expectation was integration rather than
training.

What the day a real model arrives looks like:

1. Take a published COCO-trained pose model at 192 px, export to opset 13, and check it
   against `aura_infer`'s documented operator subset. A heatmap head will not pass; a
   coordinate-regression head (an "SimpleBaseline"-style or RLE head) will.
2. **The output layout is already right.** `Joint17::ALL` is COCO order exactly, which is
   why it is written out in full rather than reduced to the seven joints this phase names -
   a replacement whose layout matches needs no decoder change.
3. Write the trained head's crop predictions into the schema printed by `python
   ml/models/composition/eval_composition.py --schema`, then run `python
   ml/models/composition/eval_composition.py --evaluate results.json`. This re-points
   section 10.1's limb-crop gate from `Analyser::with_reference_poses` at the real head.
4. Bump `MODEL_VER`, and every `image_composition` row becomes pending. `AURA-ML-5043` is
   what stops two vintages being compared while the background pass catches up.

`crates/aura-perf/tests/composition_budgets.rs` cannot assert section 11's GPU row because
this build has no GPU backend. It asserts a separate **500 ms processor-path regression
guardrail** and prints the RTX waiver on every run. The 2026-08-15 release measurement on
the development machine was **424.33 ms per image**, or **1,697 s for 4,000 images**; that
is not evidence for the 30 ms / 120 s RTX rows.

The arithmetic that makes it plausible: the convolutions dominate at roughly 96 MFLOP for
a 192 px crop, so six people in one batched call is about 580 MFLOP. **This head is the
dominant per-frame cost of the whole phase**, which is why `PoseHead::run_batch` takes a
slice and why section 9 gives PERF "share keypoint inference with Phase 06".

## Quality gate

Section 10.1's keypoint-dependent thresholds, and what each is measured against today.

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limb/joint crop detection F1 | >= 0.90 | `fixtures::cut_ladder` through `with_reference_poses` | **met (algorithms only)** |
| Top-of-head crop is context-dependent | exact | `fixtures::head_crop_pair`, two scenes | **met** |
| A cut is a crossing, not a joint near an edge | exact | `fixtures::tight_but_uncut` | **met** |
| Joint localisation error | - | - | **deferred (C1): needs a trained head** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

Read the "measured against" column. The rows marked met are real measurements of the
*audit* - the derived person box, the decoder, the joint mapping, the edge tests, the
severity arithmetic and the scene weighting - against joints that are painted into the
fixture pixels and known by construction. The localisation row is the one that needs this
model's weights, and it is deferred.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.** Everything below is about the architecture and the
   surrounding geometry, and applies equally to a trained version.
2. **The person box is derived from a face box.** Phase 06's frozen `ImageSubjects`
   carries faces and no bodies, so `keypoints::person_box` builds a box seven and a half
   head-heights tall. A seated person, a person at a strong angle and a child all get a
   box that is too tall, and **a person facing away has no face and therefore no box at
   all**. Condition C3 of the phase 11 exit report. Both fail in the safe direction: a
   wrong box produces a missing violation far more often than an invented one.
3. **Occlusion is not modelled.** A wrist behind a bouquet scores low and is treated as
   absent, which under-reports a cut rather than inventing one.
4. **Crowds.** A dance floor with twenty overlapping people produces twenty overlapping
   derived boxes, and each crop contains several people. A trained head localises the
   *most central* person in such a crop, which is usually but not always the one the box
   was derived from.
5. **int8 is forbidden here and permitted on `aesthetic_head`.** The output is a
   coordinate, and per-tensor int8 over a `0..1` coordinate is a quantisation step of about
   four thousandths of the crop - at 192 px, most of a wrist. The one decision this head
   exists to support is the one the quantisation would blur.
6. **A re-render invalidates every reading.** The head reads phase 02's documented proxy;
   a `PIPELINE_VER` bump changes the pixels it sees.

## Fallback

Complete, and it is visible rather than silent.

1. **Per frame**: a head that raises is caught by `Analyser::locate`, logged at debug, and
   the frame is judged **with no poses**. `keypoint_subjects` is zero, no cut is claimed,
   and `CompositionOutline::keypoint_aware` falls - so a wedding analysed without this
   model reports that its cropping was never checked instead of reporting that its cropping
   was fine.
2. **Per pass**: a fatal proxy, analyser, validation, or storage failure is coded
   `AURA-ML-5045` and **no row is written**, so the next pass retries it. Pose-head absence
   by itself is not fatal: a degraded row is written with `keypoints_unavailable`, zero
   keypoint subjects, and no limb-cut claim. A silently empty *clean* judgement would read
   as "this photograph is well framed" to phases 12 and 29, so the caveat is mandatory.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets a horizon, a placement, a balance, a
background reading and a composition score on every frame.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one
has completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_composition` row stale, so the next pass re-judges; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5043`), and a photographer's dismissals are replayed onto the new judgement
rather than dropped.
