## Purpose

Tell a saptapadi from a communion, and say nothing when it cannot.

This is the model behind the competitive claim in section 1 of the phase document:
global tools handle Western wedding structure adequately and Hindu, Nepali and Muslim
ceremonies poorly, and a culling engine that cannot tell a `sindoor` moment from a ring
exchange will throw one of them away. Forty-eight rites across five traditions ship in
`crates/aura-brain-wedding/config/rituals/`, and a new tradition is a file rather than a
build.

## Architecture

The same adapter shape as `scene_classifier`, one head, and one deliberate asymmetry.

```
features [N, 536] -> Gemm 192x536 -> Relu -> Gemm 160x192 -> Softmax -> ritual_probs
```

**The 160 output slots are the `RitualId`s authored in the taxonomy files.** That is the
load-bearing fact of the whole design, and it is why ADR-0015 section 3 insists the id is
written down rather than derived from file order: renumbering a rite would relabel a
trained model output. Ids are unique across every file, never reused, and
`Taxonomy::load` refuses a duplicate with `AURA-ML-5024`. Reserved ranges:

| Tradition | Ids |
|---|---|
| (reserved) | 0 - `RitualId::NONE`, the abstention |
| hindu | 10-39 |
| nepali | 40-69 |
| christian | 70-99 |
| muslim | 100-129 |
| civil | 130-159 |
| next tradition | 160 - which is a wider head, which is a retrain |

### Tradition conditioning, twice

Section 6.1 calls the head "tradition-conditioned", and it is conditioned in two places
because the two do different work:

**At training time**, through the eight-slot one-hot in the input. A `saptapadi` and a
`communion` are not competing hypotheses about the same photograph, and telling the head
which taxonomy is in play stops it spending capacity separating them.

**At decode time**, through `Taxonomy::mask_for`. The slots of rites the evidence has
ruled out are zeroed and the surviving distribution is **renormalised**. The
renormalisation is not cosmetic: without it, establishing the tradition would make the
head *less* likely to name a rite, because the floor would be applied to a distribution
that no longer sums to one. `ritual_abstention_beats_a_wrong_tradition` in
`tests/eval/scene_eval.rs` is the test that caught exactly that.

The tradition itself comes from `TraditionVote` - a vote over the rites already accepted
in the project, never from a person. `mixed` is a real answer, not a failure: an
interfaith wedding with a nikah and a signing is two traditions and calling it by the
larger one's name would mislabel half of it.

### Abstention, which is the point of the model

Two mechanisms, and they answer different questions.

**Slot 0** is `RitualId::NONE`. A head that believes there is no rite says so through the
softmax, competing with every rite on equal terms. That is the well-posed half.

**`ABSTAIN_MARGIN`** is the other half. A head split 0.36 / 0.33 between
`saptapadi_pheras` and `saat_phera` has not failed - it has correctly identified a fire
circumambulation and cannot tell which tradition's name to use. Recording either at 0.36
would put a Nepali wedding's rites under Hindu names in a client-facing timeline. Below
the margin the answer is `None` **with the reason attached**, which is invariant 2 applied
to a non-answer.

The head is also **only run where the context suggests a ceremony**
(`ritual::should_evaluate`). Running a 160-way rite classifier over a detail shot produces
an answer that is indistinguishable, once stored, from a real one.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.**

The weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a fixed seed, generated by
`aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::ritual_classifier`. It names no rite in any photograph.

This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-07-EXIT.md` and a Sev 2 trigger.**

Training this head is harder than training the scene head and the difficulty is worth
stating, because it is the part a plan usually omits:

- **The classes are extremely imbalanced.** A wedding has one `saptapadi_pheras` sequence
  and six hundred dance-floor frames. Section 6.1's answer is oversampling plus focal
  loss, and `ml/models/scene/train_ritual.py --plan` writes out the sampling schedule.
- **Some rites appear in one wedding in fifty.** `griha_pravesh`, `crowning`,
  `jumping_the_broom`. The honest target for those is a **high abstention rate**, not
  accuracy, and the eval reports abstention per rite for exactly that reason.
- **The labels need a consultant, not an annotator.** Telling a `swayambar` from a
  `jaimala_varmala` is a cultural judgement, and section 8 step 1 puts a wedding
  photographer consultant on the taxonomy before any labelling starts.
- **Consent is per wedding, per tradition.** A religious ceremony is sensitive material
  and the dataset record has to say who agreed to what.

Empty rather than plausible, as above. The head runs on a *subset* of frames - only where

## Quality gate

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ritual F1 per tradition | >= 0.85 | - | **deferred (C1): the head is a placeholder** |
| Abstention rather than a wrong label on an unseen rite | - | constructed two-tradition distributions | **met** |
| Tradition masking changes the answer | - | the same distribution with and without a tradition | **met** |
| Every fixture rite is declared by a shipped taxonomy | - | the three reference weddings | **met** |
| Taxonomy ids are unique and in range | - | all five shipped files | **met** |
| An unknown slot abstains rather than inventing a name | - | a slot no taxonomy declares | **met** |

The last one is worth reading twice. If the head and the taxonomy files come from
different versions - a trained head shipped against a taxonomy that has since gained a
rite - the decoder returns `None` with the reason "the head and the taxonomy files are
from different versions" rather than naming slot 91 something. A model that can produce a
number a table cannot interpret must not be allowed to guess.

## Known failure modes

1. **It has not been trained.**
2. **A rite it has never seen gets abstained on, not guessed at.** By design, and it is
   the difference between a useful gap and a wrong name in a client's timeline.
3. **The tradition vote is circular early in a wedding.** The first accepted rite
   establishes the tradition that masks the second. A wedding whose first confident rite
   is wrong will mask the correct tradition for the rest of the pass. Mitigated by the
   floor and the margin being high enough that a first acceptance is a strong one, and by
   `TraditionVote::verdict` returning `mixed` rather than committing when the runner-up
   holds 30 % of the votes.
4. **Adding a tradition past id 159 is a retrain.** The head's width is fixed. This is
   documented in `docs/adding-a-tradition.md` and refused by the loader rather than
   discovered later.
5. **It reads no person.** Not a failure mode - a constraint, and it is structural. The
   input is an embedding, sixteen context numbers and a tradition one-hot; there is no
   slot in which a face, a name, a skin tone or a garment could be expressed. The same
   boundary `aura_cloud::tasks::SEGMENT_NAMING_SYSTEM` draws in its third rule, drawn
   twice on purpose.
6. **int8 is forbidden.** A 160-way softmax spends most of its mass on slot 0 and the rest
   thinly, so the difference between `saptapadi_pheras` and `saat_phera` lives in the
   third decimal place. Quantising that does not make the head faster in any way a
   photographer would notice; it makes the abstention margin meaningless.

## Fairness

**Not published, and not approximated.** This is the model where the disparity would be
sharpest and it is the one with the least data behind it.

What is needed:

- **per-tradition F1 and per-tradition abstention rate**, on a set with balanced coverage
  of the five shipped traditions. Both, not either: a head that abstains on every Nepali
  rite and names every Christian one has a fine average F1 and is useless to half the
  customers this product is built for;
- the same figures on the **night** subset, since three of the five traditions hold their
  central rites after dark;
- an agreed maximum disparity, decided with PM and the consultant.

The mitigation is implemented: the margin rule turns "cannot tell which tradition's name
to use" into silence rather than into the majority tradition's name, which is precisely
the direction a fairness failure should fail in.

## Fallback

The head not running at all is a supported state. `scene::pass::run_rituals` logs and
continues; the wedding keeps its scene labels and its chapters, and every rite field is
`NULL`. `SegmentNaming` can still name a rite from a contact sheet, and the photographer
can still rename a chapter by hand.

## Rollback

As `scene_classifier`: pinned by digest, previous version retained until one real
inference succeeds, `AURA-ML-5022` on any version drift. A taxonomy edit bumps
`taxonomy_ver` alone, which re-reads slugs without re-running the head.
