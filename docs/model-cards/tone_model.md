## Purpose

Decide how much contrast, highlight recovery, shadow lift, white point and black point one
photograph wants.

PHASE-16 section 6.1: "predict the five tone parameters with a small MLP over features:
histogram percentiles, subject luminance, scene class, dynamic range, flash flag, noise
level." That is the whole of this head's job, and its boundaries are as interesting as its
job:

- **It does not emit a curve.** The curve is *solved* from the scene's contrast intent under
  three constraints, and ADR-0033 decision 2 records why: a head that emitted control points
  could emit non-monotone ones, and monotonicity is the one property of this phase that is
  structural rather than checked. `ml/models/colour/export.py` refuses a checkpoint whose
  output is not exactly five wide, so an experiment that changed the head's shape cannot
  quietly reach the product.
- **It does not emit an HSL block.** The eight bands come from what is actually in the frame -
  `colour::content` and `colour::harmony` - because a head predicting a green-band shift from
  a luminance histogram would be predicting the average wedding rather than this photograph.
- **It does not decide anything about skin.** The guarantee in section 6.3 is a measurement
  taken after the grade, and nothing this head emits can weaken it.

## Architecture

A three-layer perceptron over thirty-four numbers:

```
features [N, 34]          # 11 measurements + 23 scene one-hot
  -> Gemm 64x34 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 32x64 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 5x32  -> Sigmoid -> tone
```

**It takes no pixels**, for phase 15's `exposure_scene` reason: what it has to learn *is* a
summary - a conditional mean over twenty-three scene classes with eleven covariates. The
eleven measurements are five histogram percentiles, the dynamic range, the subject's median
and interquartile spread, the clipped and crushed fractions and the normalised shadow
headroom. All eleven are already computed by `tone::stats` and `colour::analyse` for other
reasons, so this head costs a matrix multiply and nothing else.

**The scene is a one-hot, not an index**, for the reason every conditioned head in this
product uses one: a scalar index would let the network learn an ordering between scenes that
does not exist.

**Every output is bounded.** A `Sigmoid` maps each onto plus or minus that parameter's own
limit in `colour::tone` - 50 for contrast, 60 for the two recovery controls, 40 for the two
endpoints. An unbounded regression head trained on a few thousand expert edits will
occasionally emit a contrast of 300, and a contrast of 300 applied to a ceremony is a
photograph nobody delivered.

### int8 is forbidden, and the reason is not the tolerance

Five sigmoids mapped onto the recipe's ranges means a per-tensor int8 quantisation step of
about 0.4 units of contrast. On one frame that is invisible, so the reason is not accuracy.

It is that this head would decide the grade of **every photograph in the wedding**. A
systematic bias of half a unit, applied identically to four thousand frames, is a gallery
uniformly slightly flatter than the album that was built from the same recipes at a different
precision - and phase 25 is then asked to reconcile two things that were never the same. A
quantisation error that is invisible per frame and systematic per gallery is exactly the error
a per-frame tolerance does not catch. Phase 15's two heads carry the same policy for the same
argument.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.** Its weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a
fixed seed, generated by `aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::tone_model`. Its output over a real
frame is a bounded random projection of thirty-four numbers.

Section 8 steps 1 and 2 ask to "extract tone/HSL parameters from the expert-edit dataset" and
then "train and validate the tone parameter model". That dataset does not exist here. This is
**condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-16-EXIT.md`**.

**The mitigation is structural, not a promise.** `colour::tone::TONE_HEAD_TRAINED` is `false`,
and while it is false `Analyser::tone_hint` returns `None` and the head is **never run at
all**. What decides every frame is `colour::tone::solve` - a deterministic solver over the
histogram, the subject's own spread, phase 09's noise estimate and the scene's intent row.

ADR-0033 decision 5 records why that is a decision rather than a fallback: a random projection
blended with the solver at any weight is a random contribution at that weight, and it would be
indistinguishable in the panel from a learned one. **The solver is the shipped behaviour, and
the head is the thing that will one day have to beat it.**

What the day real pairs arrive looks like:

1. Build the JSONL described by `python ml/models/colour/train_tone.py --plan`. One row per
   RAW: the eleven measurements as the product computes them, the scene label, the
   photographer, and the expert's own five parameters in the recipe's units.
2. **At least five photographers.** Two photographers grade the same ceremony differently and
   both are right; a head trained on one has learned that person's taste and will call it the
   consensus. Phase 17 is where one photographer's taste belongs.
3. **Wedding-level splits, never frame-level.** A random frame split lets the model see the
   same wedding on both sides and reports a memorisation score.
4. **A subtlety penalty in the loss.** Mean squared error alone rewards a large prediction
   when the model is unsure, because a large error on a rare heavy edit costs more than a
   small error on a common light one. `train_tone.py` sets the penalty at 0.25 against
   `SUBTLETY_CEILING`.
5. Export, then write held-out predictions into the schema printed by
   `python ml/models/colour/eval_colour.py --schema` and run it. The gate is a *comparison*:
   **a learned head that cannot beat the solver has learned nothing worth shipping.** That
   comparison is why the solver outlives the placeholder.

## Quality gate

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subject contrast within tolerance of the scene intent | >= 85 % of frames | `colour::fixtures`, subject spread painted into the pixels | **met (solver)** |
| Every generated curve monotone, no posterisation | exact, every frame | a 4,096-step ramp through the renderer's own interpolation | **met** |
| Skin hue shift, every skin tone | <= 2 deg | five reflectances, measured after the real grade | **met (solver)** |
| Skin chroma change, every skin tone | <= 6 % | as above | **met (solver)** |
| No new clipping beyond the scene tolerance | >= 99.5 % of frames | `colour::fixtures` | **met (solver)** |
| Shadow lift within phase 09's noise budget | exact, every frame | `colour::fixtures` at five headroom values | **met (solver)** |
| Greenery moves toward the scene target and is calmed | every greenery frame | foliage hue painted into the pixels | **met (solver)** |
| Tone parameter MAE against expert edits | within expert tolerance | **no expert edits exist** | **not measured** |

Every "met" above is a measurement of the **solver**, on synthetic frames whose answer was
painted in. None of them is a claim about this model's weights, and none of them is a claim
about a photograph.

## Ethical and fairness notes

The skin rows in the gate table are the ones with consequences outside the product, and there
are three things to be precise about.

**The ceilings are measured, not asserted.** `colour::skin_guard` grades this frame's own skin
pixels through the real renderer, measures the hue and chroma they moved, and re-solves - or
withdraws the colour operations entirely - until they are inside the ceilings. The measurement
is stored on the row, so the guarantee is a query rather than a sentence in a document.

**The baseline is the frame's own skin after the tone half.** Not a target, not a reference,
not a locus. There is no ideal-skin constant anywhere in this phase, no column in migration 16
for one, and the phase gate scans the schema on every run. A fixed target is how an editor
lightens dark skin while believing it is correcting a cast; a product that has nothing to
compare a person against cannot do it.

**The fairness measurement is five reflectances, not five people.** They are five points on a
line through the region human skin occupies, they live in `tests/eval/colour_eval.rs` and in
`ml/models/colour/eval_colour.py`, and nothing in the product stores a skin-tone bucket. The
gate proves the *mechanism* is self-referential and says nothing about a photograph of a real
person. That is condition C2 of the phase 16 exit report, and `docs/skin-fairness.md` says the
same thing in the product's own words.

## Known failure modes

1. **It is not trained, and while that is true it is not used.** Condition C1.
2. **It is blind to what the photograph is of, beyond its scene label.** Eleven numbers and a
   one-hot cannot tell a black tuxedo on a white sheet from a white dress on a black sheet, and
   the two want opposite tone curves. That is a real limit of the design, accepted for phase
   15's reason: the alternative is a head that spends its capacity re-deriving the scene
   classifier's job.
3. **A wrong scene label is a wrong grade, silently.** The one-hot is phase 07's answer and
   phase 07's classifier is itself a placeholder (phase 07 condition C1).
   `ColourDecision::scene` is stored on every row so the assumption is at least visible.
4. **It cannot decline.** Unlike the focus head in phase 09, which can only ever *withdraw* a
   claim, this head's five numbers would be used as a grade. That asymmetry is why every bound
   in this phase - the per-parameter clamps, the clipping guard, the subtlety cap and the skin
   guard - sits in the pipeline rather than in the head: a wrong answer here is reduced by four
   independent mechanisms before it reaches a pixel.
5. **It has never seen a photographer disagree with it.** Section 12s fourth failure mode is
   HSL and tone fighting the photographers taste, and the only cure is phase 17. Until then the
   alternatives - flatter, punchier, warmer - are what a photographer reaches for instead.

## Fallback

`colour::tone::solve`, and it is not a degraded mode: it is what ships. A deterministic solver
over the histogram percentiles, the subject spread, phase 09's measured shadow headroom and
the scene's intent row, every step of which a colour scientist wrote and a product manager
approved. The decision then carries reasons that name what moved and why, which a learned head
could not produce without a second model to explain the first.

When the head is trained, the solver stays. It is the comparison the gate is against and the
answer for a frame whose inference fails.

## Rollback

Pinned by digest in `models/models.lock` and signed in `models/manifest.sig`. A version that
fails its first real use is rolled back automatically and recorded as rejected. Because
`TONE_HEAD_TRAINED` is false, rolling this model back changes nothing that a photographer can
see - which is the clearest statement available of how much of phase 16 depends on it.
