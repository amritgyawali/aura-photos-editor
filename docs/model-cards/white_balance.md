## Purpose

Be the one white-balance hypothesis that is not a summary statistic.

PHASE-15 section 6.2 lists four hypothesis generators: grey-world, white-patch, a learned
CNN prediction, and known-neutral regions. Three of those four are arithmetic over the
frame's own statistics, and they fail on exactly the frames a wedding is made of - a bride in
a white dress breaks grey-world, a specular highlight on a champagne flute breaks white-patch,
and a reception with no white object in shot breaks the neutral detector.

The learned head exists to have a different failure mode from the other three. That is its
whole job, and it is why it reads pixels while everything else in this phase reads numbers:
what a photograph *looks like* carries evidence about what lit it - skin, wood, foliage,
tablecloths, the falloff on a wall - that no histogram retains.

## Architecture

A four-stage convolutional trunk over a 64 px linear thumbnail:

```
thumb [N, 3, 64, 64]          # linear sRGB, NOT gamma-encoded
  -> Conv 16, 3x3, stride 2 -> Relu     # 32
  -> Conv 32, 3x3, stride 2 -> Relu     # 16
  -> MaxPool 2                          # 8
  -> Conv 48, 3x3, stride 2 -> Relu     # 4
  -> GlobalAveragePool -> Flatten
  -> Gemm 64x48 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 2x64  -> Sigmoid -> illuminant_uv
```

Four decisions in that graph are the model contract rather than the architecture, and each of
them is the kind that has no code to check it:

**The input is linear.** An illuminant estimate is a statement about the *ratio* of channel
energies. A transfer curve applied before the first convolution makes that ratio depend on
how bright the frame is, so a head trained on sRGB-encoded thumbnails learns "warm and dark"
as a different thing from "warm and bright". Invariant 8, at the model boundary.
`tone::stats::FrameStats::thumbnail` is the only thing that builds this tensor, and the
manifest says `linear_srgb` so that a future exporter cannot quietly disagree.

**The output is a chromaticity, not a temperature and a tint.** A correlated colour
temperature is the projection of a chromaticity onto the Planckian locus. A fluorescent tube
sits well off that locus and a cheap LED further; their distance from it - the `Duv` - is
precisely the error a white balance has to correct, and a head trained to emit kelvin would be
trained to throw it away. `aura_raw::colour::illuminant` is the one conversion from `u'v'` to
the two numbers a slider shows.

**The output is bounded.** Across every illuminant a wedding can contain, `u'` spans roughly
0.15 to 0.32 and `v'` roughly 0.44 to 0.53. A `Sigmoid` plus a fixed rescale means this head
cannot propose a chromaticity outside the diagram, which a linear output trained on a few
thousand examples certainly would - and an impossible illuminant is worse than a wrong one,
because the solver would score it against a skin locus and reject it for the wrong reason.

**64 px, not 224.** Illuminant estimation is a low-frequency problem. Every published learned
estimator downsamples hard before its first convolution, and section 11 budgets 25 ms per
image for the whole phase.

### int8 is forbidden, and this is the sharpest case for it in the product

Section 10.1's gate is 200 K. Near daylight that is about 0.004 in `u'v'`, on an axis whose
entire useful span is about 0.17. Per-tensor int8 quantises that span into steps of roughly
0.0013, so **three quantisation steps is the whole tolerance the phase is measured against**.
`PrecisionPolicy::no_int8` is set in `xtask/src/models.rs` and the registry refuses an int8
variant.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.** Its weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a
fixed seed, generated by `aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::white_balance`. Its output over a real
photograph is a bounded random projection of a thumbnail.

Section 8 step 1 asks for "RAW + expert final edits with exposure/WB parameters across
traditions and lighting types", and section 9 budgets DATA ten days for it. There is no such
dataset in this repository, there are no camera files, and there is no photographed
ColorChecker. This is **condition C1 of `docs/progress/PHASE-15-EXIT.md`**.

**The mitigation is structural, not a promise.** `analyse::WB_HEAD_TRAINED` is `false`, and
while it is false the learned hypothesis is **never generated at all** - the solve runs on
grey-world, white-patch, the camera's as-shot value and any known neutrals, all four of which
are real arithmetic. The panel therefore never claims a learned prediction it did not make,
and no frame in this build is white-balanced by a random projection.

What the day real pairs arrive looks like:

1. Build the JSONL described by `python ml/models/tone/train_wb.py --plan`. One row per RAW:
   the 64 px linear thumbnail and the expert's own temperature and tint, converted to `u'v'`
   through the *same* function the product uses.
2. **Wedding-level splits, never frame-level.** Two frames from one reception share an
   illuminant; a frame-level split makes the held-out error meaningless.
3. **Stratify by lighting class, not by count.** Section 9 names eight lighting classes. A
   dataset that is 70 % outdoor daylight trains a head that has learned "5,500 K" and scores
   well on a test set that is also 70 % outdoor daylight.
4. Train with an angular loss in `u'v'` rather than a squared error in kelvin - see
   `train_wb.py`, which refuses to build a kelvin target at all.
5. Export, then write held-out predictions into the schema printed by
   `python ml/models/tone/eval_tone.py --schema` and run `--evaluate`. The gate compares
   against the *other three hypotheses*: **a learned head that cannot beat grey-world plus
   white-patch plus neutrals on the mixed-light subset has learned nothing worth shipping.**

these weights, because these weights are never consulted.

## Known failure modes

1. **It is not trained, and while that is true it is not used.** Condition C1.
2. **A learned illuminant is still only a hypothesis.** Even trained, this head's answer is
   scored against the skin locus and the neutrals like every other candidate, and it loses when
   it makes somebody's skin implausible. That is by design: a single-source white balance is
   what fails on the frames this phase exists for.
3. **It cannot see a region.** The output is one chromaticity for the whole frame. Mixed light
   is detected by `illuminant::split`, from block chromaticities, not by this head - so a head
   that is confidently wrong on a two-light frame is a hypothesis that loses to the split,
   rather than a frame that gets corrected as though one light lit it.
4. **A thumbnail dominated by one large coloured object is a trap.** A red sari filling the
   frame looks like red light. The skin constraint is what catches it, and on a frame with no
   identified faces there is nothing that does - which is what `ToneOutline::skin_constrained`
   exists to report.
5. **fp16 rounding is inside the tolerance and int8 is not.** See the precision note above.

## Fallback

Complete, and it is visible rather than silent. With this model absent or untrained the solve
runs on four hypotheses instead of five; `HypothesisSource::Learned` never appears in
`image_tone_estimate.illuminants`, `ToneCode::GreyWorldFallback` is emitted when grey-world
wins with no neutral to anchor it, and the white-balance confidence carries the loss.

A wedding with this model uninstalled still gets a temperature and a tint on every frame, a
mixed-light mark where there is mixed light, and the full skin constraint.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one has
completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_tone_estimate` row stale, so the next pass re-estimates; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5060`).
