## Purpose

Expose the frames that have nobody in them.

PHASE-15 section 6.1's third bullet: "when no face is present (details, venue), fall back to a
learned scene-level exposure model trained on expert edits of the same scene class." Everything
else in this phase is subject-referred - the prominence-weighted face luminance against a
scene band - and none of it applies to the rings on a windowsill, the empty ceremony hall
before anybody arrives, or four hundred flat-lays of stationery.

Those frames are a large minority of a wedding and they are the ones where a frame-referred
auto-exposure looks *worst*: a dark, moody detail shot and an underexposed one have the same
mean luminance, and the difference between them is entirely a matter of what kind of
photograph it is.

## Architecture

A three-layer perceptron over thirty-five numbers:

```
features [N, 35]          # 12 measurements + 23 scene one-hot
  -> Gemm 48x35 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 24x48 -> Relu
  -> Gemm 1x24  -> Sigmoid -> exposure
```

**It takes no pixels, and that is the interesting half of this pack.** Its sibling
`white_balance` reads a thumbnail because the colour of a light is not recoverable from any
summary of the pixels. This head reads a summary because what it has to learn *is* a summary:
a conditional mean over twenty-three scene classes with twelve covariates. Give it pixels and
it would spend most of its capacity rediscovering a median that `tone::stats` already computes
exactly.

The twelve measurements are the frame's median and mean, four percentiles, the centre and
border means, the clipped and crushed fractions, and two histogram-shape terms. All twelve are
already computed for other reasons, so this head costs a matrix multiply and nothing else.

**The scene is a one-hot, not an index.** A scalar index would let the network learn an
ordering between scenes that does not exist - that a `venue` is somehow between a `ritual` and
a `flat_lay`. Section 2.1 requires a detail shot to be exposed differently from a venue
interior, not less than one. Phase 11's `aesthetic_head` makes the same choice for the same
reason.

**The output is bounded.** A `Sigmoid` maps onto plus or minus `tone::exposure::MAX_MOVE_EV`.
An unbounded regression head trained on a few thousand expert edits will occasionally emit six
stops, and six stops applied to a venue photograph is a white rectangle.

### int8 is forbidden, and the reason is not the tolerance

One sigmoid mapped onto six stops means a per-tensor int8 quantisation step of about 0.024
stops - comfortably inside section 10.1's 0.15 EV gate. So the reason is not accuracy on one
frame.

It is that this head decides the exposure of **every faceless frame in the wedding**. A
systematic bias of two hundredths of a stop, applied identically to four hundred details and to
none of the frames with people in them, is a visible step between the chapters that have faces
in them and the ones that do not - and gallery consistency is then what phase 25 is asked to
repair. A quantisation error that is invisible per frame and systematic per chapter is exactly
the error a per-frame tolerance does not catch.

## Training data

**None. This model is not trained.** Its weights are a deterministic 64-bit xorshift from a
fixed seed, generated by `aura_infer::onnx::fixtures::exposure_scene`. Its output over a real
frame is a bounded random projection of thirty-five numbers.

Section 8 step 6 asks to "train and validate the scene-level exposure model for faceless
frames" against the dataset of step 1. That dataset does not exist here. This is **condition C1
of `docs/progress/PHASE-15-EXIT.md`**, shared with `white_balance`.

**The mitigation is structural, not a promise.** `analyse::EXPOSURE_HEAD_TRAINED` is `false`,
and while it is false the faceless path falls back to the scene's own band from
`config/exposure_targets.toml` applied to the frame's median - which is a rule a colour
scientist wrote and a product manager approved, rather than a random number. The estimate then
carries `ToneCode::NoFaceSceneModel`, so the panel says which of the two decided.

What the day real pairs arrive looks like:

1. Build the JSONL described by `python ml/models/tone/train_exposure.py --plan`. One row per
   RAW: the twelve measurements as the product computes them, the scene label, and the expert's
   own exposure offset in stops.
2. **Faceless frames only.** A training set that includes frames with people teaches this head
   to do the job the subject-referred path already does better, and it will then be *selected*
   for on exactly the frames where it is worse.
3. **Wedding-level splits, never frame-level.** Four hundred flat-lays from one wedding are one
   photographer's exposure preference sampled four hundred times.
4. **Balance the scene classes.** Twenty-three classes and a long tail: a set that is 40 %
   `venue` produces a head that has learned one number.
5. Export, then write held-out predictions into the schema printed by
   `python ml/models/tone/eval_tone.py --schema` and run `--evaluate`. The gate is a
   *comparison*: **a learned head that cannot beat the scene band applied to the median has
   learned nothing worth shipping.** That comparison is why the band outlives the placeholder.

## Quality gate

| Gate | Threshold | Measured against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure within 0.15 EV of expert | >= 85 % of frames | `tone::fixtures`, subject luminance painted into the pixels | **met (solver, face-anchored path)** |
| Faceless frames exposed sensibly | within the scene band | `fixtures::faceless_details` | **met (scene band, without this head)** |
| Clipping never increases beyond scene tolerance | exact | every fixture | **met** |
| Backlit frames expose for the subject | exact | `fixtures::backlit_exit` | **met** |
| Learned head beats the scene band | - | - | **deferred (C1): needs expert pairs** |
| Determinism | byte-identical | two runs of the fixture | **met** |

Every "met" is a statement about the **solver and the target table**, not about these weights,
because these weights are never consulted while `EXPOSURE_HEAD_TRAINED` is false.

## Known failure modes

1. **It is not trained, and while that is true it is not used.** Condition C1.
2. **It is blind to what the photograph is of, beyond its scene label.** Twelve numbers and a
   one-hot cannot tell a black tuxedo on a white sheet from a white dress on a black sheet, and
   the two want opposite exposures. That is a real limit of the design, accepted because the
   alternative - pixels - buys it at the cost of a head that would then also be re-deriving the
   scene classifier's job.
3. **A wrong scene label is a wrong exposure, silently.** The one-hot is phase 07's answer, and
   phase 07's classifier is itself a placeholder (phase 07 condition C1). `ToneEstimate::scene`
   is stored on every row so the assumption is at least visible.
4. **It cannot decline.** Unlike the focus head in phase 09, which can only ever *withdraw* a
   claim, this head's output is used as an exposure. That asymmetry is why the bound and the
   clipping clamp are in the pipeline rather than in the head: a wrong answer here is clamped to
   the scene's own tolerance before it reaches a pixel.
5. **`untargeted_scenes` is the number to watch.** A scene with no row in
   `exposure_targets.toml` gets the neutral band, and a wedding whose scenes are all missing has
   been exposed entirely against neutral. `AURA-ML-5064` and `ToneOutline::untargeted_scenes`
   report it.

## Fallback

Complete, and it is visible rather than silent. With this model absent or untrained, a faceless
frame is exposed by applying the scene's band from `config/exposure_targets.toml` to the frame's
median, `ToneCode::NoFaceSceneModel` is recorded, and the exposure confidence carries the
loss. `ToneEstimate::face_anchored` is false either way, so a caller can always tell a
subject-referred exposure from a scene-referred one.

## Rollback

`models.lock` pins by digest and the registry keeps the previous version until a new one has
completed one real inference (`AURA-ML-5009`). A `MODEL_VER` bump makes every
`image_tone_estimate` row stale, so the next pass re-estimates; two versions are never compared
(`AURA-ML-5060`).
